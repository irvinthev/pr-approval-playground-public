Underlying risk categories and control implications ▸

These overarching risks inform the specific control objectives below; they are not additional approval stages.

Unauthorized changes

Protect approval authority and enforce authorization for the precise revision. G01 · D01

Incorrect classification

Apply deterministic exclusions and resolve unknown dependencies. D02 · A01

Segregation of duties

Protect policy administration and bot identities against self-authorization. G02 · A02

AI reliability

Validate unsafe cases, model changes, and safe human fallback. A01 · A04

Configuration integrity

Version and authorize changes to rules, CI, prompts, thresholds and permissions. A03 · D03

Deployment integrity

Link approved source to artifacts and prevent alternate release bypass. P01 · P02

Monitoring gaps

Monitor overrides and reconcile the deployed population. P03 · X02

01 · Core engineering

Foundational DevOps

Controls that belong in a mature software delivery process even when no AI reviews code. Focus: source protection, authorization, tests, delivery integrity and operational evidence.

02 · Added automation risk

AI-Specific

Additional objectives when a model evaluates, recommends, approves or executes actions. Focus: reviewer quality, limited authority, model and prompt governance, and safe failure.

03 · Applied financial relevance

SOX Considerations

An additional assessment lens—not a separate universal stack of controls. Where a system affects financial reporting, evaluate authorization, independence, evidence and audit reliance.

What's different from a legacy ITGC RCM?

Shift from prescribing a specific sequence of manual tickets, reviews and CI tools to defining enforceable outcomes: authorized changes, protected policy, dependable tests, constrained automation, traceable artifacts and complete deployment paths.

One technical mechanism may satisfy several objectives. Conversely, a green check may not satisfy even one objective if its authority, completeness or operating boundary cannot be established.

Where does SOX fit?

SOX relevance is determined by whether changes affect financial-reporting processes or supporting controls—not the presence of AI alone. For financially relevant changes, management must establish an appropriate authorization mechanism and dependable evidence. External auditor reliance is a separate assessment.

AI model scoring, repository scoping and source-to-production provenance each answer different questions. None independently establish change authorization effectiveness.
