02 / SUPPORT THE CONCLUSION

What does the testing establish?

These examples help challenge design and implementation. A successful demonstration supports only the conditions tested; a conclusion about operation over a period needs more.

Evidence over time, model changes and audit reliance
  • Establish the relevant financial-reporting risk, control objective and reliance period before choosing the nature, timing and extent of testing.
  • Inspect changes and exceptions across the period, including prompts, models, tool permissions, policy, CI and release configuration.
  • Corroborate population completeness and evidence reliability; inquiry or a dashboard alone is insufficient.
  • AS 2201 .B28–.B33 describes conditional benchmarking for automated controls. Do not assume unchanged rules demonstrate unchanged AI behavior; evaluate model, prompt, vendor and input dependencies separately.
  • Management owns policy and rollout decisions. Auditors assess whether the evidence supports the specific conclusion; these examples are not a universal sample plan.

Framework references and scope

Principles are summarized and adapted to this fictional workflow. Security guidance does not by itself cover financial correctness; provenance does not prove authorization or functional correctness. The tests above are illustrative applications, not procedures prescribed by the referenced organizations.

Reference editions: NIST SSDF 1.1, AI RMF 1.0, OWASP LLM Top 10 2025 and SLSA 1.2. Check the applicable PCAOB standard version and effective date for the audit period.

Negative tests for required CI and progressive production rollout

Pre-merge challenge: Select an applicable unit, integration, E2E, financial logic and security check. Inspect the effective applicability policy and deliberately reproduce a failed or absent result on a test revision. Verify auto-approval and merge cannot proceed; inspect changes to check names, workflow definitions and privileged bypass rights.

Financial correctness: Inspect boundary, negative and reconciliation cases that correspond to financially relevant behavior. Passing tests do not establish correctness for untested cases.

Canary challenge: Reproduce a threshold breach using a controlled test or documented demonstration. Inspect the release strategy, technical/business metrics, artifact identity, promotion decision, stop/containment/rollback behavior and evidence of recovery. For unsuitable canary candidates, assess the alternative release and recovery design.

SOX-specific assurance considerations
THE PRACTITIONER’S CHALLENGE

Applying SOX assurance to agentic CI/CD

When AI agents assess code and inform automated approval, the audit question extends beyond whether a PR has an approval record. For SOX-relevant changes, practitioners must translate audit standards and technology guidance into tests of financial-reporting risk.

  • Scope: Could the change affect financial logic directly or through a dependency?
  • Authority: Who owns the decision, and who can change the agents, policy or enforcement?
  • Reliance: Do the tests and retained records support the conclusion across revisions, model changes and production routes?

Our approach: The references below provide audit, AI-risk and secure-development principles. This guide combines them with DevSecOps practices into a targeted view of the two-AI-reviewer workflow we are demonstrating. The stage mappings and tests are our application of those principles, not a prescribed SOX approval model.

PCAOB standards inform the SOX audit lens; NIST, OWASP and SLSA contribute complementary guidance. These examples do not establish compliance or control effectiveness.

Why these safeguards matter

The references below are design guidance, not a certification checklist. Select an example to connect a risk, a safeguard, an audit challenge, and the relevant published framework. The related questions are illustrative—not mandatory text from the frameworks.

AI-02 Shared dependency → eligibility

Engineering safeguard: Versioned dependency mapping; unknown or incomplete required impact routes to a human.

Assurance question: Can an excluded billing dependency be reached indirectly through a shared library, and does an unknown mapping prevent auto-approval?

Framework lens: NIST SSDF SP 800-218 — secure development practices (principle-level association; not a prescribed dependency gate).

Open control preview AI-02 → · Try connected shared-library scenario →

AI-03 AI false approval → validation

Engineering safeguard: Independently adjudicated unsafe cases, severity-aware false approvals, documented operating limits and periodic revalidation.

Assurance question: What is the false-approval rate for materially unsafe cases, rather than merely the percentage classified low risk?

Framework lens: NIST AI RMF 1.0 — MEASURE 2.1, 2.3, 2.5 (test sets, measures, validity and limitations).

AI-05 Stale approval → revision binding

Engineering safeguard: Bind the approval to commit, required base, policy and dependency mapping identities; invalidate when relevant inputs change.

Assurance question: Can an approval for an earlier revision authorize newly changed code or a changed policy context?

Framework lens: NIST SSDF SP 800-218 — supporting secure-development practice; specific revision-binding behavior is a proposed system design, not a quoted NIST requirement.

AI-07 Policy authority → administrative separation

Engineering safeguard: Independently administer approval policy, bot permissions and bypass settings; review inherited and emergency access.

Assurance question: Can a contributor alter the mechanism that approves their own changes, whether through direct or inherited privileges?

Framework lens: NIST CSF 2.0 — PR.AA-05 (permissions, least privilege and separation of duties).

Open control preview AI-07 →

AI-09 Deployable artifact → provenance

Engineering safeguard: Reconcile the reviewed commit, controlled build and deployed artifact, including alternate release paths.

Assurance question: Can a different or unapproved artifact reach production despite a valid GitHub approval?

Framework lens: SLSA v1.0 Build Track (provenance and build integrity, not a substitute for change authorization).

AI-14 Model performance → monitoring

Engineering safeguard: Monitor severity-weighted false approvals, model changes, class shifts, human escalations and suspension triggers.

Assurance question: What triggers revalidation or withdrawal of automated approval when operating conditions change?

Framework lens: NIST AI RMF 1.0 — MEASURE 2.4 (production behavior monitoring).

AI-02 A shared library affects billing

Risk: A low-risk classification misses a downstream billing dependency.

Proposed safeguard: Use a versioned service and dependency mapping. Unknown, stale or incomplete required mappings block automated approval pending assessment.

Expected evidence: Mapping version, affected services, owner-supported impact assessment and routing decision.

Challenge: Remove a required dependency mapping. Verify that the gate blocks automated approval until the impact is resolved.

Limit: Known mappings can still omit relationships. Validate them against architecture and business owners.

AI-05 Code changes after approval

Risk: An earlier approval authorizes code or policy context that was never evaluated.

Proposed safeguard: Bind approval to the current revision and required base, policy and mapping identities. Missing required identity cannot qualify as valid approval. Relevant changes invalidate approval.

Expected evidence: Revision identity, policy and mapping versions, approval record and invalidation events.

Challenge: Push another commit or change a required version. Verify the earlier approval cannot authorize the new inputs.

Limit: A PR number or branch name does not uniquely identify reviewed source.

Examine the policy gate →
AI-07 A contributor can change approval rules

Risk: Someone changes the policy, bot or enforcement to authorize their own work.

Proposed safeguard: Restrict administration of approval authority and require independent authorization for changes. Review effective and inherited access, including emergency privileges.

Expected evidence: Role assignments, app permissions, credential ownership, configuration history and authorized administrative changes.

Challenge: Test whether a contributor can alter policy, approval credentials or deployment enforcement directly or through another identity.

Limit: Different author and bot identities alone do not establish segregation of duties.

Examine approval authority →
AI-09 A different artifact reaches production

Risk: The deployed artifact does not trace to the authorized source and trusted build.

Proposed safeguard: Verify source identity, trusted build provenance and artifact digest at release. Missing or mismatched required evidence blocks release.

Expected evidence: Authorized source revision, build provenance, artifact digest and deployment manifest.

Challenge: Substitute an artifact or remove provenance in an authorized test environment. Verify release is blocked.

Limit: Provenance does not establish functional correctness or authorization by itself.
