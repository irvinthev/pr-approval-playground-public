Independent Assurance · Design challenge

How do we know AI-enabled changes remain appropriately controlled?

AI can write, review, classify and potentially authorize changes. The assurance challenge is not simply whether an AI reviewer produces a plausible result. It is whether consequential actions are appropriately scoped, authorized, enforced and supported by reliable evidence—even as models, permissions, policies and dependencies change.

An informed, risk-based proposal—not a compliance certification. This fictional, public-safe guide draws on established ITGC/SOX concepts and selected NIST, CSA, OWASP, secure-development and engineering guidance. These sources inform our questions; they do not endorse this architecture, prove effectiveness, or establish an audit opinion. Framework mappings require applicability and source verification.

How we framed the approach

Start with authority and risk, not an agent count

Our working method connects three complementary questions. It deliberately separates AI classification from the authority to approve and release a change.

01 · RISK

What must be controlled?

Identify the consequential decision, financial or security impact, applicable scope and delegated authority.

02 · ENGINEERING

Where is it enforced?

Map the relevant SDLC transition and trust boundary to preventive safeguards, qualified human routes and response.

03 · ASSURANCE

What can be demonstrated?

Test policy, behavior and evidence; reconcile populations; investigate exceptions and reconsider earlier conclusions when facts change.

One control library, multiple views. Risk domains organize objectives; the SDLC identifies operational touchpoints; protected actions and transitions make enforcement testable. There is no assumption that a new monitoring platform is necessary.

01 / Establish

What must already work?

Before relying on autonomous review, establish protected rules and bypass coverage, independently governed authority, an approved test baseline, defensible eligibility/dependency mappings, scoped agent tools and trustworthy event sources. A second AI reviewer does not substitute for any of these foundations.

For financially relevant systems, applicable ICFR and change-management requirements form the assurance starting point. Voluntary AI and security guidance adds design and testing considerations, not new automatic SOX obligations.

02 / Test

How do we challenge the safeguards?

Different assertions require different methods. Running a check for every received event does not prove the source population is complete, and running a model evaluation does not establish independent approval authority.

A · Deterministic control reperformance

Compare source-native events against protected invariants: SHA-bound approval, required checks, identity, policy context, artifact provenance, release routes and population completeness.

B · AI behavioral and adversarial evaluation

Use independently adjudicated harmful cases, correlated-failure challenges, production samples and change-triggered canaries to assess false-safe decisions. Keep model fitness distinct from authorization.

C · Independent assurance and human judgment

Assess adequacy of requirements and test oracles, source integrity, mapping completeness, exception disposition and the validity of management's monitoring system. Internal Audit evaluates rather than operates management controls.

03 / Conclude

What does the evidence actually establish?

Design

Are ownership, boundaries, permitted decisions, requirements and response paths sufficiently specified?

Implementation

Do effective configurations, permissions, tests and bypass restrictions match approved design?

Operation & reassessment

Are complete, reliable records and behavioral evidence available across the period? Do later discoveries affect prior conclusions?

Preserve, don't rewrite. If a billing dependency is discovered after deployment, retain the original authorization and contemporaneous evidence. Separately record when the fact became known, which past decisions are affected, and the accountable human disposition. Later knowledge is not automatic proof that the earlier authorization was invalid.

Indeterminate, failed, not-applicable and passed are distinct conclusions. A plausible demonstration is not operating evidence.
