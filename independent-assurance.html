ASSURANCE / A PRACTICAL FIELD GUIDE

How do we gain assurance over AI-enabled software development?

Start with the authority delegated to AI, identify what independently enforces its limits, and determine what evidence demonstrates those safeguards work. Generating code, advising reviewers and authorizing changes require different assurance approaches.

The five-part method below applies across AI-enabled software delivery models. A separate fictional case illustrates one possible automated-approval design; it is not the required architecture or a description of any company's implementation.

Follow the assurance journey: understand AI authority, evaluate foundational enforcement, validate the implementation, monitor operation and assess the evidence needed for reliance.

Illustrative guidance, not a prescribed architecture or conclusion of control effectiveness. Applicable requirements, management decisions and audit judgments depend on the actual environment.

01 / UNDERSTAND AI AUTHORITY

Know what AI does—and who controls it.

First establish what AI can do: generate code, advise a human, evaluate a change, or exercise approval authority. Then identify who or what authorizes the exact revision, how approval is recorded, and whether the merge and deployment paths enforce that authorization. Human merge alone is not substantive human review.

The sequence below is a fictional automated-approval case used throughout the interactive example, not a baseline architecture. Organizations may use one reviewer, multiple reviewers or deterministic automation depending on the authority delegated and controls in place.

Human developer creates a PR → Deterministic eligibility checks protected scope → Two AI review mechanisms assess an eligible change → AI sign-off and human merge remain separate actions → Non-AI release controls enforce deployment and traceability.

Map the company/vendor boundary before choosing evidence.

In the fictional case, a vendor-managed review service is paired with a company-configured reviewer using a vendor model/runtime. These are illustrative responsibility arrangements, not verified descriptions of specific products or a requirement to use two reviewers. For each mechanism, establish who controls the model, runtime, prompts, reviewer code, policies, permissions and input data—and what the company can inspect, test or change.

Separate mechanisms do not by themselves establish independence. Shared models, inputs, administrators or credentials may create common failure paths. Review available provider assurance for its actual scope, exceptions and customer responsibilities; validate company-controlled integration and enforcement separately. If vendor internals are unavailable, identify the limit and test observable behavior and surrounding safeguards.

Choose tests for the authority delegated.

An AI code generator calls for testing generated changes through the development and review process. An advisory reviewer calls for testing recommendation quality and how humans use it. An AI approval mechanism also calls for testing eligibility boundaries, approval credentials, revision binding, bypasses and escalation. Human merge remains a distinct action; its presence alone does not establish that a substantive human review occurred.

02 / FOUNDATIONAL ENFORCEMENT

Establish the foundations before relying on AI.

Test the safeguards appropriate to the AI's actual authority. Foundational change-management and security controls remain relevant even where AI is advisory; automated authorization adds stronger demands on eligibility, credentials, revision binding, independent enforcement and bypass prevention. Challenge what could undermine the decision: a bot changing its own rules, an administrator bypassing protection, or an incorrect dependency map.

Enforcement and bypass

Global branch protection, CODEOWNERS, required checks, emergency routes and direct-to-production access.

Authority and separation

Independent ownership of approval policy, bot logic and deployment paths; service-account privileges and access reviews.

Vendor and agent security

Shared responsibility, SOC report scope, tool permissions, prompt injection and restrictions on bot self-modification.

Trusted inputs

Repository classification, labels, tagging and dependency mappings: provenance, completeness and who can alter them.

03 / VALIDATE THE IMPLEMENTATION

How would we validate this before enabling AI approval?

Before delegating approval authority, management should demonstrate that configuration, security safeguards, reviewer behavior and escalation paths work as intended. For advisory AI, tailor tests to recommendation quality, human reliance and the surrounding workflow.

  1. Validate the configuration

    GitHub protections, agent permissions, model settings, exclusion rules, dependency mapping and approved baselines.

  2. Challenge the AI reviewers

    Known-good and known-bad PRs, incorrect labels, false-low-risk cases, correlated errors and human adjudication.

  3. Test the end-to-end workflow

    Parallel testing, failed checks, reviewer disagreements, protected changes, human escalation and downstream deployment gates.

  4. Authorize go-live

    Document test results, acceptance criteria, limitations, responsible owners, sign-offs and rollback procedures.

04 / MONITOR OPERATING EFFECTIVENESS

Continuously watch the controls, not just the bots.

Configuration drift: Changes to rulesets, CODEOWNERS, model or prompt versions, permissions, labels and dependency data must be identified and governed.

Behavioral checks: Repeated negative tests, reviewer disagreement, skipped evaluations and false-low-risk misses need expected outcomes and escalation.

Production traceability: Reconcile deployed code to its deployment job, artifact repository or registry record, source SHA and PR; tie incidents back to decisions and govern subsequent fixes.

Alert health: Monitor data freshness, missing events, alert delivery, exception ownership and the ability to suspend autonomous approval.

Ownership: First line operates and remediates; second line challenges thresholds and exceptions; third line independently evaluates control evidence. An AI assurance challenger that continuously checks expected versus observed controls is a future hypothesis; its independence, feasibility and cost remain open for discussion.

05 / EVIDENCE AND INDEPENDENT ASSURANCE

What evidence would justify reliance?

Management monitoring detects issues and produces records for response and evaluation. Available records still need checks for completeness, accuracy and retention; independent assurance evaluates whether they support the intended conclusion. Monitoring does not independently verify its own evidence. An auditor still needs complete source populations, effective historical configurations, reperformance, exception resolution and period-appropriate testing. Point-in-time testing cannot establish operation over an entire period by itself.

Design — Is the control architecture appropriate for the AI's authority and risk?

Implementation — Do the enforced settings and independent negative tests actually work?

Operation over time — Can complete, reliable evidence substantiate execution, exceptions and approved changes throughout the period?

Compliance evaluation addresses applicable control and reporting requirements; broader risk management also considers model reliability, vendor exposure, security and operational resilience. The sufficiency of each requires its own judgment.

OUR CONTRIBUTION / CONNECT THE DISCIPLINES

From established guidance to practical assurance

The challenge is not a lack of frameworks. It is translating their different purposes into a practical, defensible way to evaluate AI authority inside software delivery.

Here, AI risk guidance frames the concern, security guidance informs the threat, Engineering demonstrates a safeguard, management monitors its operation, and Audit independently evaluates the evidence.

A proposed synthesis for discussion, not a new regulatory standard, official mapping, certification, or claim that no AI audit guidance exists. Specific requirements and testing remain dependent on scope and professional judgment.
