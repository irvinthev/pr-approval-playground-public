      EXAMPLE INDEPENDENT VALIDATION APPROACH

      What would justify relying on automation?

      A practical sequence for any modernized change-management environment.

      Expand the illustrative audit approach

      1 · Establish scope and population

      Independently identify financially relevant services and production changes. Validate completeness across standard CI/CD, manual, emergency and privileged pathways before relying on dashboard counts.

      2 · Validate enforcement

      Inspect branch and deployment rules, authorization identities, administrative independence, emergency access and policy-change governance. Reperform expected denials and allowed routes with approved test evidence.

      3 · Trace the exact change

      Reconcile PR revision → applicable review/approval → build artifact → deployed production version. Investigate unmatched and superseded approvals.

      4 · Challenge AI-specific decisions (where used)

      Test deterministic exclusions, unknown dependencies, representative false-low-risk cases, reviewer-version changes, failure states and unauthorized bot actions. Accuracy supports classification; it does not itself confer approval authority.

      5 · Evaluate continuous monitoring

      Validate data source reliability, transformation logic, detection coverage, exception ownership, retention and resolution. Consider whether tested controls operated consistently over the reliance period.

      6 · Determine the testing response

      Where evidence is reliable and controls operate as designed, more targeted, exception-focused testing may be justified. Incomplete populations, bypass gaps or unstable classifier behavior call for expanded direct work. The extent of reliance remains an auditor judgment based on the applicable engagement.

      Bottom line

      Moving from manual approvals to automated delivery does not remove the authorization objective. It changes the mechanisms Audit must independently test—and may enable less repetitive transaction testing only when those mechanisms and the evidence are trustworthy.
