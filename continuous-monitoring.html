Example audit approachHow to validate deployment-population completeness and alternative production pathsExpand ↓

Objective: Determine whether the monitoring population captures production deployments across authorized and alternative pathways—not merely whether the 24 records already in the feed reconcile to themselves.

  1. Establish the routes. Obtain the documented deployment architecture, production write permissions, CI/CD configuration and emergency procedures. Identify direct cloud/API, privileged service-account, manual, break-glass and infrastructure-as-code pathways.
  2. Develop an independent population. Obtain time-bounded production activity from an appropriate source independent of the monitored CI/CD feed (for example, cloud control-plane events, release or artifact records, and runtime version changes). Validate each source's scope, retention, filters and completeness.
  3. Reconcile both directions. Match deployments to monitored records using environment, timestamp, service, artifact digest or commit identifier; investigate unmatched, duplicated or altered records. Do not assume every infrastructure event equals a code deployment.
  4. Test alternative paths. Inspect bypass identities, credentials and permissions; examine actual use and approved negative-test results where feasible. Determine whether emergency and manual production changes generate monitored evidence.
  5. Evaluate exceptions. Establish whether missing events reflect authorized exceptions, source-collection gaps or potentially unapproved changes; verify remediation and retain the original exception trail.
Illustrative result — 24 / 25

An independent release record reveals one manual deployment absent from the standard feed. The observed ratio becomes 24 of 25 known deployments. The discrepancy requires investigation; it does not alone prove an unauthorized change or establish the completeness of the expanded population.

SOX relevance: Where deployments affect financial-reporting systems, an incomplete population can undermine reliance on change-authorization controls and the completeness of monitoring evidence.

RR-02 / FROM SIGNAL TO RESPONSE

What if an unsafe change is classified low risk?

Look for a possible false-low-risk outcome when an independent test, revised dependency map, disagreement or incident conflicts with the original decision. A low-risk label alone is not an alert, and incomplete inputs may hide unsafe cases entirely.

  1. SignalCompare the decision with independent evidence for the exact PR revision.
  2. ResponseInvestigate affected scope; escalate to human review or pause automation if policy warrants.
  3. EvidencePreserve inputs, policy/model versions, comparison results, disposition and remediation.

THE PRACTITIONER’S CHALLENGE

Green checks are only part of the picture

Connect code, reviewer findings, authorization and the deployed artifact. Missing events or unowned exceptions undermine change evidence.

  • Coverage: Are all revisions, approval routes and production paths represented?
  • Judgment: Are unsafe approvals, model changes and human-review backlogs being challenged?
  • Response: Who investigates, suspends automation and authorizes recovery?

Our approach: Apply NIST AI RMF and SSDF, OWASP and SLSA principles to the two-AI-reviewer workflow we are demonstrating. PCAOB evidence principles inform the SOX lens. The signals and responses below are targeted examples, not monitoring rules prescribed by those sources.

Management owns monitoring and response. Independent assurance evaluates whether the monitoring and underlying evidence can be relied upon.

Before using these monitors

Define the population, processing window, threshold, owner and closure evidence. Combine event monitoring with periodic owner review; required preventive gates still apply.

  • Set response thresholds from the risk and service context. Investigate severe individual cases; compare trends using explicit time windows, denominators and model/policy versions.
  • Track false-low-risk approvals and unnecessary human escalations separately. A higher approval rate is not proof of better classification.
  • Monitor collection delays, source outages, filters, duplicates, retention and access to logs. Reconcile critical populations against independent sources.
  • Retain the original alert, investigation, disposition and remediation. Distinguish a confirmed deviation, a collection gap and an unresolved assessment.
  • Define suspension, rollback and restart authority; exercise them. Correcting telemetry does not establish that an unapproved deployment was authorized.

Required CI and canary outcomes — example monitoring signals

Pre-merge: Compare protected required-check definitions to actual outcomes and merged revisions. Investigate failed or absent unit, integration, E2E, financial logic, SAST, SCA, secrets and IaC checks that nonetheless permit merge.

Canary: Correlate rollout phases and artifact identity with technical error budgets, business reconciliation, promotion decisions, failed rollback and manually initiated releases. Alert when promotion proceeds after a breached threshold or when canary coverage is insufficient.

Population integrity: Reconcile against independent merge and deployment sources; a complete alert feed cannot be inferred from a green dashboard.
