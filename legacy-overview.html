The principle
AI ≠ Authority

An AI risk assessment can inform a decision. A separately governed and enforced policy determines whether the change can proceed.

Conceptual experience — not a live approval workflow
Start here / The context

What is this, and why does it matter?

Software teams want to ship routine changes faster. AI tools can now examine pull requests, flag potential defects, and recommend whether a change looks low risk. The harder question is whether that recommendation should ever become permission to merge or deploy without a person approving it.

The opportunity

Less waiting, more engineering time

A narrow, well-governed automated path could reduce review queues for routine, eligible changes while routing sensitive or uncertain work to people.

The problem

A green score is not authorization

AI can miss a downstream dependency or misread a change. An approval bot can also have excessive permissions, stale decisions, or bypass paths. Review quality and decision authority are different questions.

The purpose

Explore where the safeguards belong

This interactive walkthrough lets you inspect each decision point and test fictional changes to see when an automated path may proceed and when it should stop.

A 30-second example

Two AI reviewers say “low risk.”

A developer changes a shared software function. Both automated reviews pass, but that function feeds a billing calculation. Should the PR be approved automatically? This experience shows why the answer depends on scope, independent policy, and enforceable controls—not just two green reviews.
