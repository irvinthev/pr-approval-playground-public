NIST AI RMF: A practical approach

Use four connected functions to structure assessment, from governance and risk identification through validation and ongoing response.

♙Govern

Set policy, ownership, accountability and risk tolerance.

◎Map

Understand the AI system, data flow, affected assets and credible risk scenarios.

⌕Measure

Evaluate risk, safeguard design, testing, evidence and uncertainty.

⚙Manage

Choose treatment, monitor operation, escalate and reassess.

The four RMF functions are not stages that must occur only once or in this order. This site's risks, safeguards and classifications are illustrative adaptations.

Risk assessment guidance / NIST AI RMF–informed

From AI authority to a risk assessment

NIST AI RMF 1.0 organizes AI risk management into Govern, Map, Measure, and Manage. The four functions inform this practical method; they are not scoring bands, a prescribed control checklist, or a certification. The scenarios and RCM mappings below are field-guide adaptations, not official NIST risk statements or descriptions of implemented controls.

  1. GovernIdentify accountable owners, authority, policy boundaries and tolerance.
  2. MapDocument AI participation, intended use, criticality, affected systems, failure modes and affected parties.
  3. MeasureValidate classifications, negative cases, control evidence, gaps and uncertainty.
  4. ManageDetermine permissible authority, safeguards, escalation, monitoring and reassessment triggers.

Assessment sequence: Scope the operating model → select credible failure scenarios → identify independently enforced safeguards → validate evidence → record open questions → have management evaluate impact, likelihood, tolerance and response. Missing evidence means not assessed, not low risk. Risk rankings require a management-approved scale and context; this guide intentionally does not calculate them.

Reference: NIST AI RMF 1.0 ↗ and NIST AI RMF Playbook ↗.

AI inventory → data and crown jewels → risk ownership

Use a draft worksheet to record AI systems, accountable owners, use cases, information classification and critical-asset exposure; then track scenario-level risks and proposed treatment. Data classification identifies sensitivity of information; crown-jewel assessment identifies business assets whose compromise or disruption could have disproportionate consequences. These concepts must be assessed separately.

    Map participation and authority

    For each stage, document whether activity is human-led, AI-assisted, AI-executed, or conventional deterministic automation. Then identify effective permissions and independent enforcement. These categories are descriptive—not a Low/Medium/High risk scale.

    SDLC activityQuestion to answerAuthority to examine
    DevelopCan AI alter code, tests, dependencies or configuration?Execution; tool and repository permissions
    ReviewDoes AI recommend findings or determine whether a required check passes?Decision influence; reviewer reliability
    ApproveCan AI output authorize merge without a human?Delegated authorization; independent policy enforcement
    DeployDoes AI choose or initiate a release, or does deterministic CI/CD execute an approved release?Production execution and release decision
    Monitor / remediateDoes AI only identify issues, or may it change production state?Advisory, remediation and containment permissions

    Four authority dimensions

    Execution

    What can it change?

    Source, tests, tools, configuration, and workflows.

    Decision

    What can it determine?

    Review findings, eligibility, authorization.

    Production

    What can it release?

    Deployments, rollbacks, autonomous remediation.

    Governance

    Can it change the rules?

    Policies, thresholds, identities, prompts, models, enforcement.

    From exposure to an enforceable policy

    1. 1. MapActivities and effective authority
    2. 2. AssessCriticality, failure pathways and consequences
    3. 3. ChallengeApplicable safeguards and supporting evidence
    4. 4. DecideManagement tolerance and eligibility
    5. 5. EnforcePermit, human review, block or suspend

    Do not infer a residual-risk rating from AI participation alone. Missing reliability or enforcement evidence results in an undetermined conclusion—not a favorable default.

    Risk questions that change with authority

    Operating patternKey concernSafeguard emphasis
    AI develops; human approvesDefects, unsafe tool use and automation biasConstrained execution, independent testing, meaningful review
    Human develops; AI authorizesFalse approvals, missed impact, stale verdictsValidated classification, protected eligibility, exact-revision enforcement
    AI develops, authorizes and deploysCommon-mode errors and cross-stage propagationIndependent enforcement, restricted production authority, containment and recovery

    These are illustrative comparisons. None establishes inherent likelihood, effective controls, or residual risk in a real environment.

    Translate risk tolerance into decisions

    Management owns the approved scope and boundaries; technical enforcement determines whether each condition holds. Keep these outcomes separate: eligible for conditional authorization, qualified human authorization required, blocked for failed mandatory conditions, and AI capability suspended when its control path is unreliable.
