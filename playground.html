The design in plain language
Three questions. Separate authority.
Is it technically eligible?
Deterministic screening, CI and AI assessments identify whether a change can proceed through the modeled technical route.
What authorization is required?
A separately governed policy maps affected components to protected financial processes and required reviewers.
Does this revision satisfy it?
Enforcement checks the exact revision, required approvals and applicable policy versions before allowing the modeled route.
A favorable AI classification cannot waive a protected review requirement. A financial assessment cannot override failed technical checks.
Who controls the gate?
In the proposed design, requesting teams and approval bots cannot unilaterally change its rules or satisfy its review requirements. Infrastructure administrators must also lack unilateral control over its code, identities and enforcement for that independence claim to hold.
What does the calculator add?
Conditional exposure estimates can inform review priority and scope. Protected financial logic can require review even when dollar exposure is unknown or small.
What remains uncertain?
Mappings can be incomplete or wrong. A real implementation needs independently governed administration, verified lineage, trustworthy evidence and coverage of deployment bypasses. A client-side simulation establishes none of those controls.
All examples are invented. No company-specific data, credentials, live systems or proprietary configurations are used.

You built this for Engineering. Audit can use it too.
These same operational capabilities can form the foundation for change traceability, exception monitoring, evidence of deployment completeness, and incident impact assessment.


What is still needed for assurance?
Operational usefulness does not automatically establish control effectiveness. An auditor would still need to evaluate:
Authorization: Was the exact source version appropriately approved, and was approval independently enforced?
Completeness and accuracy: Are all relevant deployments and incidents represented reliably?
Governance: Who can disable monitoring, change policy, or bypass CI/CD?
Financial relevance: Did this incident affect a process or system supporting financial reporting?
Takeaway: You didn't build this for Audit. You built it because good Engineering needed it anyway.

What remains to establish audit reliance?
Authorization: Evidence that each access grant was approved for a legitimate role.
Completeness: The repository, team, service-account and token populations are complete.
Independence: Developers cannot silently alter required reviews, policy checks or bot privileges to approve their own work.
Operating effectiveness: Permissions, CODEOWNERS requirements and monitoring were actually enforced throughout the period.
CODEOWNERS determines who is requested or required to review code. It does not, by itself, authorize repository access or prevent privileged bypass.

Two paths to an authorized change




Human-review path
Developer authors the change → qualified reviewer independently reviews → CI passes → protected merge → deployment.
Strength: human judgment with relevant context. Limitations: reviewer fatigue, variable rigor, and slower turnaround.


Automated-approval path
Developer authors → deterministic eligibility passes → AI risk evaluation is favorable → governed approval authority → protected merge → deployment.
Strength: repeatable routing for narrowly eligible changes. Limitations: incomplete scope, correlated model errors, and privileged automation.
