1. Change Eligibility & Impact

01What evidence establishes the full downstream and financial impact of each candidate change?＋

External benchmarks — what they say

B07: OpenAI Codex; Anthropic Claude Review — Scoped AGENTS.md or CLAUDE.md and REVIEW.md encode local invariants and review guidance that generic models may miss.

B07 ↗

Example design (fictional)

Illustrative design: repo and path allowlists, with dependency mappings provided as inputs to automated eligibility.

Potential failure / unresolved assumption

A supposedly low-risk change could affect billing, entitlements, privacy or critical shared services through an undocumented consumer.

Question to Engineering

How can a shared-library change qualify when the downstream dependency graph is incomplete, outdated or contradictory?

Evidence that would resolve the challenge

Authoritative service/dependency owners, versioned mapping, missing/stale-map failure exercises, billing-consumer false-omission test.

02Which change classes must remain ineligible for automated approval, independent of model confidence?＋

External benchmarks — what they say

B05: OpenAI OSS Codex practice — OpenAI reports using automated review for repetitive correctness while keeping architectural, cross-team, API-compatibility and release decisions human.

B05 ↗

Example design (fictional)

Illustrative design: a deterministic exclusion list and two LOW AI classifications determine eligible PRs.

Potential failure / unresolved assumption

A code-correct PR might alter contract semantics or production behavior requiring domain-owner authorization.

Question to Engineering

Can a LOW-rated change affecting billing event meaning, permissions or backward compatibility still bypass qualified human review?

Evidence that would resolve the challenge

Approved exclusions and risk taxonomy; deliberately misleading descriptions and semantic contract-change tests.

2. AI Classification & Validation

03How reliably does the AI classifier classify consequential changes as ineligible for automated approval?＋

External benchmarks — what they say

B01: OpenAI Codex — Known rule violations AND safe counterexamples; measure coverage, restraint, retention and actionable findings.

A12: NIST AI RMF — MEASURE 2.5 — The AI system to be deployed should be demonstrated valid and reliable; limitations of generalizing beyond development conditions should be documented.

B01 ↗ · A12 ↗

Example design (fictional)

Illustrative design: positive/negative golden tests and two AI reviewers are used before a bot may approve.

Potential failure / unresolved assumption

Both models can assign LOW to materially risky PRs; passing rule tests does not measure false-low-risk outcomes.

Question to Engineering

What independent human-labeled test set and acceptance evidence support LOW classifications on the implementation's actual PR population?

Evidence that would resolve the challenge

Shadow PR adjudication, seeded unsafe and safe counterexamples, by-severity false-low misses, risk acceptance limits.

04How do we detect shared AI blind spots and degraded classification performance over time?＋

External benchmarks — what they say

B06: Anthropic Claude Code Review — Multiple agents review in parallel; findings are checked against actual code and severity ranked. Standard product comments; it does NOT approve/block PRs.

A13: NIST AI RMF — MEASURE 2.6 — The AI system should be evaluated regularly for safety risk, operate within residual-risk tolerance, fail safely outside knowledge limits, and have meaningful monitoring and response.

B06 ↗ · A13 ↗

Example design (fictional)

Illustrative design: parallel AI assessments plus periodic review and monitoring.

Potential failure / unresolved assumption

AI agreement may reflect the same missing data or prompt blind spot; performance may drift after activation.

Question to Engineering

How does Engineering detect two agreeing but wrong LOW verdicts, and what signals suspend automated approval?

Evidence that would resolve the challenge

Paired-model adjudication, difficult dependency cases, thresholds by severity, production drift monitoring and stop conditions.

3. Approval Authority & Independence

05Can automated approval be invoked only within explicitly delegated authority and without self-approval or bypass?＋

External benchmarks — what they say

B09: OpenAI agent guardrails — Agent-system guidance describes explicit human review for ambiguous/high-risk actions and independent fail-closed enforcement; not a native GitHub approval standard.

B09 ↗

Example design (fictional)

Illustrative design: bot approval, protected branches and scoped merge identities.

Potential failure / unresolved assumption

Model scores may be mistaken for independent authorization; privileged bot/merge actors could exceed approved scope.

Question to Engineering

Who is authorized to approve which classes, and what prevents the author, bot or bypass identity from overriding required human review?

Evidence that would resolve the challenge

Actual GitHub rulesets/CODEOWNERS, bot role scopes, independent admin review, bypass/emergency failure tests.

06Does any reviewer failure or disagreement always route to real qualified human approval?＋

External benchmarks — what they say

B03: Cursor PR Routing & Approval — Waits ~8 minutes for running enabled checks, then does not approve; but may decide WITHOUT findings when reviewer has not started or skipped latest commit.

B09: OpenAI agent guardrails — Agent-system guidance describes explicit human review for ambiguous/high-risk actions and independent fail-closed enforcement; not a native GitHub approval standard.

B03 ↗ · B09 ↗

Example design (fictional)

Illustrative design: both AI reviewers must return LOW; uncertain outcomes route to a human.

Potential failure / unresolved assumption

Never-started or stale reviewer signals might be treated as approval, or fallback might be only another AI pass.

Question to Engineering

Can the PR merge when either reviewer is absent, skipped, stale, contradictory or returns malformed output?

Evidence that would resolve the challenge

Never-started/skipped/timeout/stale-SHA tests; bot and privileged merge outcomes; actual human CODEOWNER escalation.

4. Policy & Configuration Governance

07Can eligibility, model, prompt, required checks or approval policy be weakened without independent authorization?＋

External benchmarks — what they say

B02: Cursor PR Routing & Approval — Specific policy files override generic criteria; ambiguous specificity routes conservatively; proposed policy edits cannot relax review for their own PR.

B02 ↗

Example design (fictional)

Illustrative design: versioned risk policy and configuration reviewed through standard pull requests.

Potential failure / unresolved assumption

A change could relax the very policy governing its authorization; drift in prompts, thresholds or exclusions can widen eligibility.

Question to Engineering

Which approved base policy evaluates a PR changing its own approval rules, and who independently authorizes policy/model changes?

Evidence that would resolve the challenge

Base-version policy binding, protected config, dual control, rule-change alerts, self-relaxation and admin-bypass tests.

5. Security & Agent Trust Boundaries

08Can untrusted PR instructions manipulate model output or cause unauthorized tool actions?＋

External benchmarks — what they say

C12: OWASP LLM01:2025 — Prompt Injection — OWASP describes unintended model behavior caused by untrusted instructions; recommends distinguishing untrusted content, least privilege, high-risk human authorization and adversarial testing.

B08: Anthropic Claude Code Action security — Warns about untrusted PR input, bot triggers, base-ref config, short-lived minimally scoped tokens, secret exposure and unsafe checkout.

C12 ↗ · B08 ↗

Example design (fictional)

Illustrative design: AI inspects PR diffs/comments and informs an automated approval decision.

Potential failure / unresolved assumption

Prompt injection could bias LOW decisions or invoke tools with elevated credentials.

Question to Engineering

What separates untrusted PR text from privileged decision logic, tool invocation and credentials?

Evidence that would resolve the challenge

Attack-driven testing, explicit trust boundary, constrained tool invocation, sanitized logs and Security review.

09Are bot permissions and third-party AI data flows constrained to their authorized purposes?＋

External benchmarks — what they say

C13: OWASP LLM06:2025 — Excessive Agency — OWASP attributes harmful agent behavior to excess functionality, permissions or autonomy; recommends reducing each and using independently constrained actions.

B08: Anthropic Claude Code Action security — Warns about untrusted PR input, bot triggers, base-ref config, short-lived minimally scoped tokens, secret exposure and unsafe checkout.

C13 ↗ · B08 ↗

Example design (fictional)

Illustrative design: separate identities for model review, approval and merge operations.

Potential failure / unresolved assumption

Overprivileged agents, tokens or data exposure could undermine independence and compromise IP or secrets.

Question to Engineering

Which precise identities, privileges, secrets and third-party data flows are necessary, and what independent security baseline approves them?

Evidence that would resolve the challenge

Effective GitHub app scopes, least-privilege matrix, secret protections, vendor/data-flow review, negative tests.

10Do deterministic CI, security, and human-approval requirements stay mandatory regardless of AI opinion?＋

External benchmarks — what they say

B04: OpenAI Codex — Mechanical checks belong in CI; branch protections and required approvals remain hard controls outside model review.

D12: NIST SP 800-218 — SSDF v1.1 — NIST recommends embedding secure software development practices in the SDLC, including software testing and vulnerability remediation.

B04 ↗ · D12 ↗

Example design (fictional)

Illustrative design: required CI checks and a privileged automated approval path.

Potential failure / unresolved assumption

Favorable AI classifications may coexist with missing/skipped required tests or ruleset bypass.

Question to Engineering

Can two LOW decisions or an exempt merge actor ever pass if a required CI/security/integration check is absent or fails?

Evidence that would resolve the challenge

Engineering-approved test matrix by repository; branch-protection rules; failing/skipped suite and bypass demonstrations.

11Is the final merged and deployed revision the exact change that was approved and tested?＋

External benchmarks — what they say

B10: Anthropic Claude Code Review — Product exposes once-on-open vs on-every-push review triggers, highlighting explicit freshness configuration and repeated-review cost tradeoffs.

D12: NIST SP 800-218 — SSDF v1.1 — NIST recommends embedding secure software development practices in the SDLC, including software testing and vulnerability remediation.

B10 ↗ · D12 ↗

Example design (fictional)

Illustrative design: reviews bind to a commit SHA and merge queue may change the effective revision.

Potential failure / unresolved assumption

Stale AI or CI approval can survive a revision change; production may receive an unreviewed artifact.

Question to Engineering

What invalidates approvals on push/rebase/queue updates, and how is source SHA bound to the production artifact?

Evidence that would resolve the challenge

SHA A→B, merge-queue revision and policy-version tests; artifact digest; independent deploy-lineage check.

7. Monitoring, Response & Accountability

12Can Engineering independently detect all auto-approved merges, bypasses and deployed changes in near-real time?＋

External benchmarks — what they say

B11: OpenAI Codex; Cursor approval routing — OpenAI Codex describes evidence-backed test/terminal execution; Cursor ties routing to current review/policy state. Neither specifies an adopter's monitoring architecture.

E12: NIST AI RMF — MEASURE 2.4 / MEASURE 1.3 — NIST describes monitoring AI behavior in production and involving independent/domain experts in regular assessments, tailored to risk tolerance.

B11 ↗ · E12 ↗

Example design (fictional)

Illustrative design: approval decisions, Git events and deploy events feed central monitoring.

Potential failure / unresolved assumption

The AI's logs may be incomplete or mutable, and missing approval events may not appear in sampled AI decisions.

Question to Engineering

How can Engineering prove a complete, near-real-time population independent of the AI classifier's own records?

Evidence that would resolve the challenge

GitHub ruleset/bypass events + merge population reconciliation, artifact/deploy feed, missing-event alert tests.

13Are shutdown authority, escalation, ongoing sampling and residual-risk ownership operationally established?＋

External benchmarks — what they say

A13: NIST AI RMF — MEASURE 2.6 — The AI system should be evaluated regularly for safety risk, operate within residual-risk tolerance, fail safely outside knowledge limits, and have meaningful monitoring and response.

E13: NIST AI RMF — MANAGE 2.4 — NIST calls for assigned mechanisms to disengage, disable, or supersede AI systems whose behavior conflicts with intended use.

A13 ↗ · E13 ↗

Example design (fictional)

Illustrative design: model monitoring and fail-closed mechanisms are expected to support suspension and recovery.

Potential failure / unresolved assumption

Automated approvals could continue after a serious miss; disputed recommendations can linger without decision owner.

Question to Engineering

Who suspends automated approval, under what measurable triggers, and who formally accepts any remaining exposure?

Evidence that would resolve the challenge

Runbook and permissions, kill-switch dry run, decision RACI, incident escalation, post-release control-effectiveness sampling.

Boundary: All workflow examples on this public playground are fictional. External descriptions are condensed paraphrases, not exact prescriptions, proof of a deficiency, or vendor endorsements of autonomous authorization. Applicable law, security policy and SOX obligations are evaluated separately.

Engineering response options: demonstrate an existing safeguard; demonstrate an effective alternative; establish evidence-based non-applicability; or document a properly authorized residual-risk decision where permitted. “Not a SOX violation” alone does not address the engineering risk.
