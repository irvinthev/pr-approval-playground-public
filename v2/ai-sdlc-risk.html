02 / EXPOSE · Risk & Governance

Two LOW verdicts. One shared dependency.

A small-looking PR can alter a business-critical function through a dependency neither model considered. The risk is not the LOW label itself—it is the decision a system might make because of it.

The exposure

CASE-SHARED-01 changes a shared deduplication library. The fictional dependency chain reaches usage, billing and reporting.

Management policy coverage and the current dependency map are assumptions to validate, not facts the AI can invent.

FOLLOW A PLAUSIBLE FAILURE

What breaks the approval decision?

Select a failure mechanism. The impact and management decision change even when two AI reviewers agree.

DECISION TAKEAWAY

A risk label is not a policy decision. Management should define exposure, permitted authority, exceptions and escalation; Engineering then makes those constraints effective.

FOLLOW THE CONSEQUENCE

Understand the dependency before selecting a safeguard.

A change to lib/dedup/key.js can propagate to usage aggregation, billing calculations and reporting. That chain is deliberately fictional, but it illustrates why a restricted-file list may miss indirect critical-function impact. The wider risk taxonomy also covers defective AI-generated code, data disclosure, tool misuse, prompt injection and software supply-chain compromise.
