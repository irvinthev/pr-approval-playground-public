2. AI Classification & Validation

03How reliably does the AI classifier classify consequential changes as ineligible for automated approval?＋

External benchmarks — what they say

B01: OpenAI Codex — Known rule violations AND safe counterexamples; measure coverage, restraint, retention and actionable findings.

A12: NIST AI RMF — MEASURE 2.5 — The AI system to be deployed should be demonstrated valid and reliable; limitations of generalizing beyond development conditions should be documented.

B01 ↗ · A12 ↗

Example design (fictional)

Illustrative design: positive/negative golden tests and two AI reviewers are used before a bot may approve.

Potential failure / unresolved assumption

Both models can assign LOW to materially risky PRs; passing rule tests does not measure false-low-risk outcomes.

Question to Engineering

What independent human-labeled test set and acceptance evidence support LOW classifications on the implementation's actual PR population?

Evidence that would resolve the challenge

Shadow PR adjudication, seeded unsafe and safe counterexamples, by-severity false-low misses, risk acceptance limits.

04How do we detect shared AI blind spots and degraded classification performance over time?＋

External benchmarks — what they say

B06: Anthropic Claude Code Review — Multiple agents review in parallel; findings are checked against actual code and severity ranked. Standard product comments; it does NOT approve/block PRs.

A13: NIST AI RMF — MEASURE 2.6 — The AI system should be evaluated regularly for safety risk, operate within residual-risk tolerance, fail safely outside knowledge limits, and have meaningful monitoring and response.

B06 ↗ · A13 ↗

Example design (fictional)

Illustrative design: parallel AI assessments plus periodic review and monitoring.

Potential failure / unresolved assumption

AI agreement may reflect the same missing data or prompt blind spot; performance may drift after activation.

Question to Engineering

How does Engineering detect two agreeing but wrong LOW verdicts, and what signals suspend automated approval?

Evidence that would resolve the challenge

Paired-model adjudication, difficult dependency cases, thresholds by severity, production drift monitoring and stop conditions.

4. Policy & Configuration Governance

07Can eligibility, model, prompt, required checks or approval policy be weakened without independent authorization?＋

External benchmarks — what they say

B02: Cursor PR Routing & Approval — Specific policy files override generic criteria; ambiguous specificity routes conservatively; proposed policy edits cannot relax review for their own PR.

B02 ↗

Example design (fictional)

Illustrative design: versioned risk policy and configuration reviewed through standard pull requests.

Potential failure / unresolved assumption

A change could relax the very policy governing its authorization; drift in prompts, thresholds or exclusions can widen eligibility.

Question to Engineering

Which approved base policy evaluates a PR changing its own approval rules, and who independently authorizes policy/model changes?

Evidence that would resolve the challenge

Base-version policy binding, protected config, dual control, rule-change alerts, self-relaxation and admin-bypass tests.

04 / SUPPORT THE CONCLUSION

What does the testing establish?

These examples help challenge design and implementation. A successful demonstration supports only the conditions tested; a conclusion about operation over a period needs more.

Evidence over time, model changes and audit reliance
  • Establish the relevant financial-reporting risk, control objective and reliance period before choosing the nature, timing and extent of testing.
  • Inspect changes and exceptions across the period, including prompts, models, tool permissions, policy, CI and release configuration.
  • Corroborate population completeness and evidence reliability; inquiry or a dashboard alone is insufficient.
  • AS 2201 .B28–.B33 describes conditional benchmarking for automated controls. Do not assume unchanged rules demonstrate unchanged AI behavior; evaluate model, prompt, vendor and input dependencies separately.
  • Management owns policy and rollout decisions. Auditors assess whether the evidence supports the specific conclusion; these examples are not a universal sample plan.

Framework references and scope

Principles are summarized and adapted to this fictional workflow. Security guidance does not by itself cover financial correctness; provenance does not prove authorization or functional correctness. Reference editions: NIST SSDF 1.1, AI RMF 1.0, OWASP LLM Top 10 2025 and SLSA 1.2. Check the applicable PCAOB standard version and effective date for the audit period.

Negative tests for required CI and progressive production rollout

Pre-merge challenge: Select an applicable unit, integration, E2E, financial logic and security check. Inspect the effective applicability policy and deliberately reproduce a failed or absent result on a test revision. Verify auto-approval and merge cannot proceed; inspect changes to check names, workflow definitions and privileged bypass rights.

Financial correctness: Inspect boundary, negative and reconciliation cases that correspond to financially relevant behavior. Passing tests do not establish correctness for untested cases.

Canary challenge: Reproduce a threshold breach using a controlled test or documented demonstration. Inspect the release strategy, technical/business metrics, artifact identity, promotion decision, stop/containment/rollback behavior and evidence of recovery. For unsuitable canary candidates, assess the alternative release and recovery design.

SOX-specific assurance considerations
THE PRACTITIONER’S CHALLENGE

Applying SOX assurance to agentic CI/CD

When AI agents assess code and inform automated approval, the audit question extends beyond whether a PR has an approval record. For SOX-relevant changes, practitioners must translate audit standards and technology guidance into tests of financial-reporting risk.

  • Scope: Could the change affect financial logic directly or through a dependency?
  • Authority: Who owns the decision, and who can change the agents, policy or enforcement?
  • Reliance: Do the tests and retained records support the conclusion across revisions, model changes and production routes?

Our approach: The references below provide audit, AI-risk and secure-development principles. This guide combines them with DevSecOps practices into a targeted view of the two-AI-reviewer workflow we are demonstrating. The stage mappings and tests are our application of those principles, not a prescribed SOX approval model.

PCAOB standards inform the SOX audit lens; NIST, OWASP and SLSA contribute complementary guidance. These examples do not establish compliance or control effectiveness.
